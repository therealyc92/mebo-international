2025 International Diabetic Lower Extremity Symposium: Global Experts Lead Innovation in Diabetic Foot Ulcer Care

October 23, 2025|Posted by: MEBO International
GLOBAL PROGRAMSDIABETIC FOOTINDONESIASYMPOSIUM

From October 22 to 23, 2025, the International Diabetic Lower Extremity Symposium (IDLES) — hosted by the International Society of Regenerative Medicine and Wound Repair and co-organized by the Indonesian General Surgeons Society and the National Rongxiang Xu Foundation — was successfully held in Bali, Indonesia. Focused on academic innovation in diabetic foot ulcer care, the symposium gathered more than 300 experts from over 20 countries, including China, the United States, the United Kingdom, Indonesia, and New Zealand.

The symposium opened with a traditional Indonesian welcome dance. Opening speeches were delivered by Professor Aristidis Veves, President of IDLES 2025 and Director of the Rongxiang Xu, MD Center for Regenerative Therapeutics at Harvard Medical School; Professor Heri Setyanto, President of the Indonesian General Surgeons Society; and Dr. Li Li, Chair of the National Rongxiang Xu Foundation — recognizing the symposium's key role in promoting innovation in diabetic foot ulcer treatment and strengthening global academic collaboration.

Over two days, the symposium held ten specialized sessions covering the full chain of diabetic foot ulcer diagnosis and treatment: treatment pathways, multidisciplinary collaboration, advanced therapeutic technologies, country-specific opportunities and challenges in wound care, and the latest clinical trial results.

At the main venue, Professor Solomon Tesfaye analyzed 'Pathways to Amputation: Can Diabetic Peripheral Neuropathy Be Reversed by Emerging Treatments?'; Professor Marc Schermerhorn detailed new diagnostic guidelines for peripheral arterial disease in diabetic foot ulcer; and Professor John Giurini focused on 'The Road to Limb Salvage: The Podiatrist's Role'. Professor Veves discussed new treatment directions grounded in pathophysiology, Professor Thanh Dinh analyzed surgical approaches, Professor Mark Wyers introduced minimally invasive revascularization options, and Professor Sammy Dowlatshahi systematically reviewed the reconstruction ladder, sparking lively discussion.

During the critical phase of APEC 2025, the symposium specially held an APEC Dialogue session, where experts from China, New Zealand, and Indonesia presented national wound care practices, further strengthening regional medical exchange and cooperation.

The symposium also introduced dedicated Indonesia and China sessions for the first time. In the Indonesia session, experts shared experience applying regenerative medical technology to wounds in tropical regions and promoted simplified protocols adapted for primary care; in the China session, experts presented results on integrative prevention and treatment of diabetic foot ulcer and limb-salvage strategies — offering a 'China solution' for global wound care.
