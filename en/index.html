Our Science

Regeneration, Not Just Coverage

A physiological moist environment that supports the body’s own repair process — in chronic wounds and in burns.

Physiological Moist Environment

Maintains an optimal environment for cell regeneration, preventing wound bed desiccation and reducing pain.

Cellular Regeneration

Stimulates keratinocyte migration and re-epithelialization, promoting the natural restoration of damaged skin.

Reduced Scarring

Physiological regeneration significantly reduces scar tissue formation compared to conventional methods.

Clinical Focus

Chronic Wounds First

In Latin America our clinical focus starts where the daily burden is highest — diabetic foot ulcers and pressure injuries — with burn care as the founding indication.

Diabetic foot ulcers precede roughly 85% of diabetes-related amputations, and 15–25% of people with diabetes will develop one in their lifetime.

  • Maintains a physiological moist environment for repair
  • Supports autolytic debridement without damaging viable tissue
  • Topical, easy to integrate into an individualised plan
Evidence at a glance
MEBT/MEBO in the treatment of diabetic foot — randomised, controlled (n = 120)
100%Total effective rate at day 60 (vs 81.67%)
56.4 dMean healing time (vs 58.2 d)
5.88Scar score after healing (vs 7.58)lower is better

Liu Y, Wang Y, Shan P. The Chinese Journal of Burns Wounds & Surface Ulcers 2019;31(3).

Comparator: soft silicone dressing; Wagner grade 1–4; p < 0.05 on all outcomes.

Stage III–IV pressure injuries are chronic refractory wounds with high recurrence, long healing times and a heavy cost burden for bedridden and elderly patients.

  • Promotes granulation and re-epithelialisation
  • Fewer dressing changes than silver alginate in the study below
  • Substantially lower treatment cost per case
Evidence at a glance
MEBO + MEBO Wound & Ulcer Dressing in stage III & IV pressure ulcers — randomised (n = 124)
98.3%Healing rate at 2 months (vs 92.2%)
43 vs 47Mean number of dressing changes
$157Mean treatment cost (vs $391)USD per case

Chen X, Tao S, Xu H, Ji X. The Chinese Journal of Burns Wounds & Surface Ulcers 2017;29(5).

Comparator: silver alginate dressing; healing time 46.0 vs 48.3 days; p < 0.05.

Burns remain our founding indication, where MEBO has been compared with silver sulphadiazine for decades and is used across partial- and deep-thickness injuries.

  • Immediate analgesic effect during dressing changes
  • Physiological moist environment instead of dry exposure
  • Reduced scar formation versus conventional dressings

Comprehensive Care

A Wound Care Program, Not Just a Product

Clinicians in the region tell us the real opportunity is a complete pathway. MEBO is designed to sit inside it.

Etiology assessment → wound bed preparation → exudate control → infection prevention → nutritional support → treatment of underlying disease.
01

Etiology assessment

Identify why the wound is not healing before choosing a dressing.

02

Wound bed preparation

Gentle debridement and protection of viable tissue at every dressing change.

03

Exudate control

A balanced moist environment that manages exudate without desiccation.

04

Infection prevention

Reduced bioburden through moist-environment care and consistent technique.

05

Nutritional support

Systemic support as a condition for local healing.

06

Underlying disease

Glycaemic control, offloading and pressure relief alongside local therapy.
